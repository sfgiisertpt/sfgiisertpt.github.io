An introductory workshop jointly organised by the Star & Planet Formation Group at IISER Tirupati and IIT Tirupati, designed to make the fundamental concepts of astronomy and astrophysics accessible to undergraduate students and curious minds from both institutions. The sessions covered a wide range of topics — from the life-cycle of stars and nebular chemistry to the mechanics of planet formation in protoplanetary disks.

Participants had the opportunity to engage with hands-on activities, including guided night-sky observation sessions using telescopes set up on campus. Interactive Q&A discussions with research scholars allowed attendees to get a realistic picture of what it means to pursue a career in astrophysics research. The workshop was met with great enthusiasm and inspired several participants to explore further study in the field.

Public Lecture Series: How Stars and Planets are Born

✨ Photo coming soon

This upcoming public lecture series will offer accessible, jargon-free talks on one of the most fundamental questions in astronomy — how do stars and their planetary companions come to exist? Drawing on the group's current research, speakers will walk the audience through the journey from cold molecular clouds to fully-formed solar systems, including our own neighbourhood in the Milky Way.

The lectures are designed to be enjoyed by anyone with curiosity about the night sky, regardless of scientific background. Each session will include a live Q&A with the researchers, as well as a short stargazing segment weather permitting. Details on dates and registration will be announced via the group's social channels shortly.

Astronomy Awareness for School Students

This outreach initiative brings our researchers directly into secondary schools across the Tirupati region to spark a passion for science at an early age. Sessions introduce students to the wonders of the night sky — constellations, planets visible to the naked eye, and the stories behind what we see — before diving into more exploratory topics such as how telescopes work and what astronomers actually do in their day-to-day research.

Students get the rare chance to interact directly with PhD scholars and ask candid questions about a career in science. Hands-on demos — including model solar systems and live image-processing of real astronomical data — make the sessions both educational and memorable. The programme aims to reach students from varied socioeconomic backgrounds, helping broaden access to science education in the region.
